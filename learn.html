What We Covered

Workshop Curriculum

A look at everything we've covered across our journalism workshop series.

01

The Importance of Youth Journalism

Students explored the fundamentals of journalsm and the unique role student journalists have in their community. From activities exploring what makes a story newsworthy to examples of impactful journalism in the Baltimore community, students expanded their understanding of what journalism could be. This session included guest speakers from the Northwestern University Medill School of Journalism.

View Materials
02

Reporting & Research

This session focused on building strong stories from the ground up. Students learned how to develop story ideas, conduct background research, identify sources, and prepare for interviews. We practiced writing interview questions and discussed how to find public records and data that bring stories to life. This session included guest speakers from the Baltimore Sun and CBS News.

View Materials
03

Writing Your Story

Students dove into the craft of writing — from structuring a news article using the inverted pyramid to writing compelling ledes that hook a reader. We covered quotes, transitions, and how to balance multiple perspectives. Each student drafted and peer-edited a short news article by the end of the session.

View Materials
04

Visual Storytelling

Photography, video, and design are core to modern journalism. This workshop introduced students to the basics of photo composition, ethical photo editing, and how to use visuals to strengthen a story's impact. Students practiced shooting on their phones and discussed how images can inform or mislead an audience.

View Materials
05

Podcasting & Audio

Audio journalism opens doors to deeply personal storytelling. Students learned how to record clean audio, structure an audio piece, and edit a short podcast segment. We listened to examples from youth-produced podcasts and discussed what makes audio stories resonate with listeners in a different way than text. This session was co-hosted by the PBS NewsHour Student Reporting Labs.

View Materials
06

Student Press Rights

Understanding your legal rights as a student journalist is essential. This workshop covered Maryland's student press freedom laws, the New Voices Act, and constitutional protections against censorship. Students learned what school administrators can and cannot restrict, and how to advocate for a free student press at their school.

View Materials
07

Launching Your Outlet

The capstone session brought everything together. Students mapped out what it takes to start and sustain a student news organization — choosing a platform, building a team, setting an editorial policy, and finding funding. Groups presented their ideas for student outlets they want to create in their own schools and communities.
